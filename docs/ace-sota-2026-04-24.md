# ACE State of the Art, 2023-2026 — Research Brief for StemScriber

**Date:** 2026-04-24
**Scope:** Audio ACE, 2023-2026.
**TL;DR:** Not 5 years behind. Roughly 1-2 years behind the research frontier on triads, meaningfully behind on large-vocab sevenths, ahead of every shipping OSS library on bass-root accuracy. No MIT/Apache pretrained model beats your stem-aware detector end-to-end today — but ChordFormer-class conformer architectures will get there within 12 months.

**Subjectivity ceiling** (Koops et al., JNMR 2019; Humphrey-Bello 2015): inter-annotator agreement is **~73% MajMin** and **~54% for complex labels**; Billboard root agreement is **~76%**. Credible published "ceiling" on Billboard MajMin is roughly **85-87%** — ChordFormer (84.1%) is already at it. On Sevenths the ceiling is closer to **75-78%**; the LLM-CoT paper claims 74.6% which is essentially at the ceiling.

## 2. Answers to the specific questions

### Q1 — Who is SOTA?
Three 2025 clusters: (1) **Conformer + large-vocab + reweighted loss** — ChordFormer is the published high-water mark on the Humphrey-Bello split (83.6 MIREX, 72.3 Sevenths, 65.3 Tetrads); the ISMIR 2025 Consonance-based work is the same family. (2) **Pipeline + LLM post-processor** — arXiv 2509.18700 runs GPT-4o over MIR tool outputs and lifts a 301-class baseline by 1-3 MIREX points. Architecturally: exactly what StemScriber does, just with a bigger head. (3) **Foundation-model probing** — MusicFM and MERT are competitive on sequence-level tasks but explicitly "not SOTA" on frame-level chord recognition as of late 2024. BTC (2019) is no longer SOTA but is the most-used baseline because it is MIT-licensed and "good enough" for triads.

### Q2 — Drop-in open-source?
- **BTC** (MIT): weaknesses are (a) pretrained weights degrade outside pop (Winterreise 77% MajMin vs AAM synthetic 97%); (b) 25-class public checkpoint only; (c) no segmentation head; (d) full-mix only. You know all four.
- **Chordino**: **GPL-3.0 — commercially poisonous.** 2010-era accuracy (~72% MajMin). Skip.
- **madmom DeepChroma**: **BSD-3-Clause** (safe). HMM over chroma, ~74-77% MajMin, no sevenths. Baseline only.
- **autochord**: **Apache-2.0**, 67.3%, 25-class. Too weak.
- **Chord-CNN-LSTM / ChordMini stack**: effectively MIT (verify top-level LICENSE before shipping). 301-class, pretrained. Best commercially-safe large-vocab option today.
- **ChordFormer**: **CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 — prohibited for commercial use.** Re-implement the architecture if you want it.
- **Harmony Transformer v2**: no LICENSE file. Treat as all-rights-reserved.

Net: the only safe pretrained checkpoints are BTC and the ISMIR 2019 large-vocab repo. Everything newer with numbers is license-tainted or unreleased.

### Q3 — Is stem-aware a known winner?
Mixed. Ko (UW-Madison, 2022) found Demucs stems **hurt** a monolithic deep ACE vs full-mix, because separation artifacts leak. But: that was a single model trained on mix vs stems, not a bass-first cascade; every shipping commercial product (Chord-AI, Moises, newer Chordify) uses source separation; the LLM-CoT paper pipelines separation too. **Stem-aware is the commercial consensus, not the benchmark consensus.** Academic benchmarks reward single-model architectures on full-mix Billboard. Your bass-root-first approach is a minority-but-growing pattern.

### Q4 — Hard cases
- **Dense jazz (Peg)**: nobody solves this from audio alone. Sevenths accuracy tops at 72-75% on Billboard/Humphrey-Bello — and those are pop sets, not jazz. No published Steely-Dan-hard benchmark exists. Root-only bail on dense jazz is consistent with the field.
- **7ths-throughout (Jamiroquai)**: no published "song-consistent extended harmony" detector. Your global-7th-lock has no published equivalent; closest analog is ChordFormer's reweighted-loss, which biases training toward rare classes but does not adapt at inference. This is novel.
- **Section labeling for uniform progressions**: Harmony Transformer (Chen-Su 2019) was the first to fuse segmentation with recognition; v2 extends it. Beyond that, nothing mainstream treats "same 4 chords repeat" differently from "4 chords change every bar." Wide open.

### Q5 — Ceiling
- MajMin on Billboard: subjectivity ceiling ~85-87%. ChordFormer 84.1%. Saturated.
- MajMinInv: ~78-82% (inversions drive annotator disagreement).
- Sevenths: ceiling ~75-78%. ChordFormer 72.3%, LLM-CoT 74.6%. ~3-5pt headroom.
- Tetrads: ceiling ~65-68%. ChordFormer 65.3%. At ceiling.
- MIREX composite: ChordFormer 83.6%, ~3pt from ceiling.

Unlimited research budget on audio-only ACE buys maybe 3-4 points on Sevenths and 1-2 on MajMin before you hit human disagreement. The lever is no longer accuracy — it is *which* annotation convention you align to.

### Q6 — Drop-in to beat you?
**No commercially-safe model would meaningfully outperform you end-to-end today** if your bass-first + global-7th-lock is working. Caveats: the Chord-CNN-LSTM / ChordMini stack is a plausible drop-in for your 337-class matcher (~79-81% MIREX, verify license). ChordFormer would win but is CC-BY-NC-SA. LLM-CoT is a framework, not weights — no license issue, just OpenAI cost.

## 3. What SOTA does that stem-aware-bass-root-first doesn't

1. **Joint root+bass+pitch-class prediction with structured decoding** (ChordFormer, Discord-to-Harmony 2025). Decomposed heads + frequency-reweighted loss handle long-tail qualities (dim7, hdim7, aug) no template matcher can reach no matter how many templates.
2. **Class-reweighted loss.** Your matcher treats every class uniformly; every modern ACE loss is frequency-rebalanced.
3. **Segmentation as a first-class head** (Harmony Transformer family). Your bleed-simplifier does this post-hoc; modern models fuse segmentation + recognition in one pass.
4. **Reproducible benchmark.** Humphrey-Bello 1,217-song split with published metrics. You have a small calibration set.
5. **Consonance-aware training** (ISMIR 2025). Cmaj7→Cmaj penalised less than Cmaj→F#maj.

## 4. What stem-aware-bass-root-first does that SOTA doesn't

1. **Bass-stem-first root extraction.** No published ACE paper does this. Commercial products do, but don't publish. Product moat.
2. **Global-progression-style lock** (Jamiroquai fix). Published ACE runs frame-independent or local-window. A whole-song harmonic-style prior ("this song is in min7 land, don't downgrade") appears in no paper I found. Novel.
3. **Bleed simplification.** Published models eat the bleed; your pipeline cleans it.
4. **Template-matcher interpretability.** A 337-class match is explainable to a musician. A conformer logit isn't.
5. **Product-grade latency budget.** ACE papers don't report inference time. You ship on Modal at $0.06/song.

## 5. Three concrete options for StemScriber post-launch

### Option A — Stay put, instrument the gap (RECOMMENDED pre-launch)
Don't swap models before May 12. Your detector is within 2-3 points of what any commercially-safe OSS model would give on MajMin, and ahead on bass. Instead:
- Build a 50-100 song labelled eval set. Use `mir_eval.chord` (MIT) to compute MajMin / Sevenths / MIREX / Root / Bass.
- Benchmark against BTC (MIT) and Chord-CNN-LSTM on that set. Free labor, blog post, marketing.
- Ship.

Cost: ~2 days. Risk: lowest. Upside: you know exactly where you sit vs BTC.

### Option B — Hybrid ensemble with BTC or Chord-CNN-LSTM (June-July)
Run BTC (MIT, 25-class) or ISMIR2019 large-vocab (MIT-ish, 301-class) in parallel. Agreement → high confidence; disagreement → your logic wins on bass but adopts the neural model's quality when template confidence is low. LLM-CoT pattern minus the LLM.
Cost: 1-2 weeks. Risk: medium (license diligence on 301-class repo). Upside: +3-5 points on dense-harmony songs like Peg without losing your bass moat.

### Option C — Train your own Conformer (Q3 2026)
Reimplement ChordFormer's architecture from the arXiv paper (architecture isn't copyrighted, only weights are CC-BY-NC-SA). Train on Billboard + Isophonics + your ground-truth set. Feed it from your separated stems — the "other-stem input" nobody has tried.
Cost: 4-6 weeks + $200-500 A100 on Modal. Risk: high. Upside: best-in-class commercially-unencumbered ACE, ISMIR 2027 paper.

**Recommendation: A now, B in June, C only if B's error analysis points at the template matcher (not the chroma front-end) as the bottleneck.**
